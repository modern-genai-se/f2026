## What to prepare

**Slides.** Cover the problem the paper addresses, why it matters, what the
authors did, what they found, and what the limitations are. Assume everyone has
read the paper. Do not walk through it section by section.

**Discussion questions.** Prepare questions that cannot be answered by pointing
at a sentence in the paper. Good questions ask about design tradeoffs, threats
to validity, what the result implies for practice, or what you would do
differently.

## Logistics

Send me your slides by email by noon on the Thursday before your session. Five
points are deducted per day late, up to a maximum of 20 points.

## Citation

If your presentation includes text, figures, data, or ideas from anywhere other
than the assigned paper, cite the source. This includes other papers, blog
posts, talks, and any figure or illustration you did not create yourself. See
the [academic integrity](syllabus.md#academic-integrity) and
[generative AI](syllabus.md#generative-ai-policy) policies.
